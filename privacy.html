Blip: Screen Time Game — Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 2, 2026

This policy covers the Blip: Screen Time Game app for iPhone and the Blip website at gamesbyvidal.github.io/lifetime. Blip is made by Matthew Vidal ("I", "me").

The short version

  • There are no accounts. I never see your Blip, your check-ins, or your screen time.
  • Everything about your Blip is saved only on your own phone.
  • I count how many people use Blip and which features they use, without cookies and without knowing who anyone is.
  • I don't show ads, sell data, or track you across other apps or websites.

What stays on your phone

Your Blip's name and look, your daily screen-time goal, the screen-time numbers you type in each day, and your Blip's life story are stored only on your device. They are not sent to me or anyone else.

If you use "Copy backup code," the code contains your saved game. It goes wherever you choose to paste it, such as your Notes app or an email to yourself. I never receive it.

Deleting the app, or clearing the website's data in your browser, erases your Blip for good unless you kept a backup code.

Usage counts

Blip uses GoatCounter, a privacy-friendly counting service, to tell me how many people open the app and which features get used, for example "a check-in was finished" or "the share button was tapped." This helps me fix problems and decide what to improve.

GoatCounter does not use cookies, does not build a profile of you, and does not store your IP address. For each visit it records the screen or feature used, the general type of device and browser, screen size, country, and the website that linked you (if any). I only see totals, not individual people. You can read GoatCounter's own policy at goatcounter.com/help/privacy.

Sharing your Blip

When you tap Share, Blip makes a link and a short message. The link contains your Blip's name, age, look, life stats, streak, and a few life highlights, so anyone you send it to can see your Blip. You choose who to send it to through your phone's normal share menu; I don't see who receives it.

Tip: your Blip's name is part of the link, so it's best not to use your own full name.

Morning reminder (iPhone app)

If you turn it on, Blip shows a notification on your phone when your Blip's next year is ready. It is created on your phone, never includes your screen time or results, and can be turned off in Settings.

Daily reminder (website only)

On the website you can add a daily reminder to your calendar. This creates a calendar event on your own device or Google Calendar. I don't see your calendar.

What I don't collect

Blip does not ask for or collect your name, email, phone number, location, contacts, or any payment details. There is no sign-in. Blip cannot see your photos: if you tap Save picture, iPhone asks once whether Blip may add pictures to Photos, and Blip only adds the picture you chose to save.

Time back can optionally ask your age to show how much time you could get back over your life. You can skip it. If you enter it, it stays on your phone and is never sent to me or to GoatCounter.

Purchases

If Blip offers optional items to buy in the future, purchases are handled entirely by Apple. I never see your card or payment details. Apple's privacy policy covers those payments.

Automatic screen time (iPhone app)

If you turn on automatic tracking, Blip uses Apple's Screen Time tools to see how much of your daily goal you have used. You approve this with Face ID or your passcode. Blip then counts your whole day's phone use, across all apps and websites, the same total iPhone's Screen Time shows. You can turn it off at any time in Blip's settings or your iPhone's Settings, and Blip goes back to letting you type it in. You can also keep typing your screen time in by hand.

Apple designed these tools so that apps never see which apps or websites you use. Blip only learns one number a day: how far your total has reached, rounded down to a step of a few minutes (never more precise than 5 minutes). Screen Time's raw daily numbers are kept on your phone for about a week, so missed days can still be played, and are then deleted. When a day becomes a year of your Blip's life, that day's number is saved in your Blip's life story on your phone, so totals like your time saved keep adding up. It stays there until you delete your Blip or the app. These numbers are used only to run your Blip's life.

  • Your screen-time information never leaves your phone. It is not sent to me, to GoatCounter, or to anyone else.
  • It is never sold, never used for advertising, and never shared with data brokers or any other company.
  • Automatic tracking is free. It is never locked behind a purchase.

Once any day of your Blip's life has come from Screen Time, if you choose to share your Blip, the share message and link show your Blip's story, such as its age, job, and home. They never include your minutes, your streak, whether you hit your goal on a given day, or the apps you used.

Children

Blip is made for teens and adults and is not directed at children under 13. Blip does not collect personal information from anyone, including children. If you believe a child has sent me personal information, contact me and I will delete it.

Your choices and rights

Because your Blip lives only on your device, you are always in control: you can delete it at any time by deleting the app or clearing the website's data. If you have a question or a privacy request, contact me using the details below. Depending on where you live, such as the EU, UK, or California, you may have extra rights over your data; I will honor them, though I hold almost nothing about you.

Changes to this policy

If I change this policy, I will update the date at the top and post the new version at the same link.
